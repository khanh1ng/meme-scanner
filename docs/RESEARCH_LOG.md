# Research log: from a hindsight-biased Sharpe of 1.07 to a point-in-time baseline

This project set out to catch meme-stock squeezes before they run. Its most useful output is a
catalogue of the ways the backtest lied, each found, measured and turned into a guard. This log
records the path in order. Numbers marked *(recorded)* were measured in the research notebooks in
`archive/` and are quoted as recorded; numbers with a report and section are quoted exactly from
the reports in `docs/reports/`; numbers marked *(reproduced)* are regenerated by
`scripts/run_backtest.py` from the cached data.

## The central error

The first universe came from asking a language model to name potential meme stocks. A model
trained after 2021 already knows which stocks became meme stocks, so the request returned the
answer rather than a screen. Every backtest built on it inherited the bias. Rebuilding the universe
point in time is what turned a profitable backtest into a losing one.

> A model may **process** the past. It must never **recall** the past.

The model now only converts existing posts into numbers, only for names the deterministic pipeline
already selected, and it is off by default (`src/memescan/llm.py`).

## What the measurements say about the thesis

From the research notebooks, on the survivor universe *(recorded; to be re-measured on the full
point-in-time listing)*:

* **The squeeze thesis is inverted in this data.** Days-to-cover ≥ 3 has a lift of 0.60x on the
  chance of a +100% move within 20 sessions; below 2 it is 1.44x. The median change in short
  interest before a large move is 0%. Short interest stays a diagnostic, not a weight, until
  borrow-fee history exists.
* **Volume is normal on the day a +100% move begins** (median relative volume 0.97x). What precedes
  it is a slow build (20/60-day volume ratio 1.28x). A volume-spike trigger is structurally late. The
  July report's event study aligns on a different event, ignition, and averages rather than takes
  medians: there the spike comes on the day before ignition (mean relative volume 7.9).
* **The names that explode and the names that can absorb capital are nearly disjoint.** Median
  20-day dollar volume before a +100% move is $861k; requiring $1M halves the hit rate. This is a
  capacity ceiling, not a data gap.
* **Win rate is the wrong objective.** Trend-break exits average +30.4% and hard-stop exits −11.8%
  (August report, Section 5.1): at those two payoffs break-even sits at a 28% win rate, and every
  filter that raised the win rate lowered total profit.
* **Stops do not cap losses on these names.** Realised hard-stop losses averaged −11.8% against a
  nominal −8%, worst −39.1%: overnight gaps and LULD halts. Only position size limits the damage.

## What comes next

The specification in `docs/spec/pipeline_spec.pdf` defines the experiment that can settle the
question: a point-in-time listing that includes delisted names (from FINRA snapshots), a 7-fold
calendar walk-forward with a purge gap, a random-names floor, a SPY benchmark, and six acceptance
criteria fixed before the run (beat SPY's Sharpe of 0.95; positive in 5 of 7 folds; beat the random
floor by two standard errors; profitable without the top 10 trades; beta below 0.3; costs under a
third of gross expectancy). It has not been run.
